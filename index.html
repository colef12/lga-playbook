How to use this Playbook

The strip above reads LGA's ATIS, opens the matching page, and checks it against the SOP selection table. Each page covers selection criteria, clearance, departures, missed approach, airspace, and the effect of JFK's configuration.

RVR below 1,200 ft (CAT III): LGA is closed. For the automatic SOP check, this page treats VMC as ceiling ≥ 1,000 ft and visibility ≥ 3 SM.

Global overrides

  • JFK landing ILS 13L forces LGA to land ILS 13 (page 6). Every other page is invalid while JFK is on ILS 13L.
  • Avoid departing runway 22 as much as practical. It only appears on page 9.
  • If a selection conflicts with JFK and JFK can't adjust for wind or weather, LGA adjusts.
  • Local notifies N90 before a configuration change. N90 has final approval when it conflicts with another airport.
  • On a change, Local names the last departure in the old configuration and gets a release for the first one in the new configuration.
  • No arrivals to intersecting runways. LAHSO is not authorized.

Clearance rules on every page

  • Initial altitude 5,000 ft, or the cruise altitude if lower.
  • MASPETH climb, CONEY climb, and any RNAV SID: "climb via SID." Everything else: "maintain 5,000."
  • Aircraft that can't accept any departure procedure: clear via radar vectors to the exit gate. Local assigns the heading.
  • DIXIE filers route via the JFK VOR when LGA departs 4 or 31, or doesn't own Coney.
  • Climbs and RNAV SIDs track different courses. Delivery tells Local about any aircraft on a climb while RNAV SIDs are in use, and the reverse.
  • Departures to EWR, JFK, TEB or HPN stay on the ground until the destination tower approves an APREQ.
  • Departure frequency: the first staffed of LGA Departure 120.40, HAARP 120.80, New York Center 56 125.325.

VFR Departures — Depart runway 4

LGA is departing runway 4.

  • LGA sits inside the New York Class B: every VFR departure gets a discrete squawk and a clearance out of the Class B.

3. Skyline Route

  • The route: a VFR transition over the Hudson between the Verrazano Bridge and the Alpine Tower. It needs a Class B clearance. Southbound aircraft fly the west bank; northbound fly the east bank. Altitude 1,300–2,000.

Ground — Preferred taxi routes

The SOP's preferred taxi routes depend on the Depart / Land configuration and on which side of the airport the aircraft is on. They are a guide: weigh the traffic and issue what works.

Which side

  • West side: west of runway 4/22. Terminal A (the Marine Air Terminal, Spirit) and the west ramps.
  • East side: east of runway 4/22. Terminal B (both concourses, all airlines except Spirit and Delta), Terminal C (Delta), and the remote stands.
  • On Page 10, aircraft on the ground get their side from where they're parked. Prefiles and arrivals have no stand yet, so the side is predicted from the airline: Spirit (NKS) West, everyone else East.

DepartLandInboundOutboundInboundOutbound

Depart 22 has no row in the SOP table.

Ground rules that go with it

  • Taxiways A and B generally run in opposite directions, set by the configuration and where landing traffic will exit. Ground and Local agree how landing aircraft will taxi clear of the runway before the handoff to Ground, to keep the turnoffs clear.
  • Departing 13 and landing 4 or 22: Ground may cross runway 4/22 without coordinating with Local if the crossing traffic is holding short or can cross expeditiously, starts crossing before the arrival reaches a 2-mile final, and finishes by a 1-mile final. Local can suspend this at any time.
  • Sequence departures by alternating gate, then by alternating exit fix, then by performance (fastest first).
